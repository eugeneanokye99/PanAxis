Rearranging a factory is one of the few improvement projects you cannot easily undo. Once machines are bolted down and services are re-routed, a bad decision is costly.

That is why every Pan Axis redesign includes a step most layout projects skip: **we test it first**.

## A full shift in seconds

We build a computer model of your factory — machines, cycle times, breakdowns, people, forklifts and routes. The model runs a full eight-hour shift in seconds, for the current layout and for each new option.

You watch both side by side: shift clock, parts finished, and how busy each resource is.

## Calibrated against reality

A model is only useful if it is right. Before testing any new layout, we check that the model of your *current* floor produces the output your factory actually produces. Only then do we trust it with the options.

## Finding where the bottleneck moves

Every improvement moves the bottleneck somewhere else. In one example, the current layout's constraint was the forklift — busy 100% of the shift. After the redesign, the constraint moved to the milling machine, and output rose from 60 to 75 parts per shift.

That is useful twice: you see the gain, and you know exactly what to improve next.

## Same machines, same people

The headline result in that example was +25% output — with the same machines and the same people. The only thing that changed was where things stood.
