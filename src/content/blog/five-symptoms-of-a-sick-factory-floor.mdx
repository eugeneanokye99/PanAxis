When you feel sick, you go to a doctor. When your factory is sick, the symptoms are just as recognisable — but they are often treated one at a time, with overtime, extra forklifts or another machine.

Here are five symptoms we see again and again on factory floors, and why they so often point to the same cause.

## 1. Orders are late, but everyone is busy

If people are working hard and output still falls short, the work is probably spending more time *waiting* and *travelling* than being processed. Busy is not the same as productive.

## 2. Machines sit idle waiting for material

A machine that waits for a forklift is a machine you paid for and cannot use. In one example model, the forklift was busy 100% of the shift — and every machine queued behind it.

## 3. The floor feels crowded

Work-in-progress piles up where flow breaks down. Crowded aisles are usually a sign of stations placed in the wrong order, not a sign that you need a bigger building.

## 4. Products go back and forth

Trace one product from raw stock to dispatch. If its route crosses itself or doubles back, you are paying for every extra metre. We regularly measure parts that travel 191 m for a job that needs 80 m.

## 5. Nobody can say why

Perhaps the most telling symptom: the factory grew one machine at a time, and no one has ever drawn how work actually moves.
