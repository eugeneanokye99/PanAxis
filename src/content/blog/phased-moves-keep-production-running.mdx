The most common objection we hear is simple: *"We can't stop production to rearrange the floor."*

You shouldn't have to. A good move plan is designed as carefully as the layout itself.

## Break the move into phases

Instead of one big move, we group machines into phases that can each be completed in a weekend:

- **Phase 1** — move a few machines (for example cutting and turning)
- **Phase 2** — move the next group (milling and welding)
- **Phase 3** — finish and tidy up (packing and walkways)

Between phases, the factory runs normally.

## Sequence by dependency

Each phase is chosen so that the factory works after every weekend — even if the layout is only partly finished. Utilities, foundations and lifting equipment are planned ahead so no weekend overruns.

## Bring your team along

People who work the floor every day know things no drawing shows. We present the plan to your team before anything moves, and we hand over a report they can use long after we leave.

## Measure again

Four to eight weeks after the final phase, we come back and measure: parts per shift, travel per part, route length. You get a before-and-after you can show your board.
